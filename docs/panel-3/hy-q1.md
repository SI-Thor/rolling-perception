## 1. Which protocol deserves the centre

**Keep the shipped multi-producer Disruptor as the centre, and amend DESIGN.md to bless it — but only after the two gaps below are closed.** The argument is the junction census, not taste.

The cascade’s stage-to-stage spine inside one lane is *not* where the multi-producer (MP) pressure lives. Ingest→reflex, reflex→embed (the gate’s nominated set), refine→publish, and publish→that stream’s ledger writer are all single-producer/single-consumer (SPSC) in the built pipeline: `rolling-pipeline` drives its six rings strictly sequentially, one claim per tick per edge, so each ring has exactly one producer and one consumer thread in practice. Those junctions do not need, and do not benefit from, a general MP claim protocol.

The genuinely MP junctions are the two the question names, and both are **edge** shapes, not the spine:

1. **Tile workers → refine.** Several tile-embed producers feed one sheaf-refine consumer that must gather a frame’s tile set before it can take the Laplacian step. This is a many-to-one *join*, not a stream. The consumer does not need per-producer ordering; it needs every tile for a frame, keyed by `(frame, tile)`, and it already buffers per frame. A claim/finalize ring where producers independently claim a slot and publish, and the single consumer walks a cursor of next-sequence and waits for publication, delivers exactly that. The Disruptor’s claim uniqueness is already proven (`multi_producer_claims_are_unique_and_all_publish`, 4 threads × 50 claims, cap 8). In-order consumption is a consumer-side cursor the pipeline already hand-rolls; it is not a ring-protocol feature the SPSC design would have given for free here, because the join does not care about claim order within a frame.

2. **Streams → ledger/attention writer.** N stream lanes publish change verdicts into one cross-stream feed/ledger consumer. This is MP by construction and is the attention plane’s whole point. A shared claim ring with a single feed-writer consumer is the natural centre for this junction; an SPSC ring would need N separate rings plus a merger, which is more moving parts for the same gather.

So the centre should be the protocol that already matches the *hard* junctions and is already carrying a live 48 h soak: the multi-producer Disruptor. The SPSC design’s promised centre features — consumer acquire/release, drop policies, conservation — were **never built** (D1, D3, D4, P12 all UNPROVEN). Building it now would mean a second ring implementation, a re-run of the soak, and an edge merger at every MP junction, to buy invariants that are, at the spine, unused. The house rule is inventory before building; the shipped ring is the inventory, and its module doc in `substrate.rs` is already the correct protocol spec. Amend DESIGN.md §3.2 to say so, and record the divergences (D1, D3, D4, P12) as debts against the shipped ring, not as reasons to fork it.

Two debts must be paid on the kept ring before it is fully blessed, because the design’s intent was right even where its protocol was wrong:

- **Consumer-side delivery contract.** The ring has no consumer protocol object; consumers poll `is_published` and `unsafe read_published`. Add a consumer cursor type that waits for the next sequence to be published, reads, and releases — i.e. give the shipped ring the *consumer half* of the designed acquire/release semantics, without changing the producer claim/finalize core. That pins in-order exactly-once delivery (currently a GAP, P11) and is the minimal amendment.
- **Conservation + accounted drop.** `lease()` is block-only spin; there are no drop counters (P12). The ingest taxonomy requires shed policies (cameras drop frames, live drops stale segments) and “every shed is an events.jsonl entry.” Add a `DropNewest/DropOldest` policy per ring and a `produced == consumed + dropped + in_flight` counter, asserted at shutdown. This is a feature on the Disruptor, not a reason to replace it.

## 2. The measurement that would settle it

If I am wrong, this is the experiment that flips me to “build the SPSC ring as designed, MP at the edges.”

**Experiment.** On the Thor, run the live harbor-watch lane in its true MP configuration: 4 tile-embed workers (the count that matches the 4×4 grid’s nominated-tile parallelism) feeding one refine stage through a single shared ring, at the Slice-1 acceptance cadence (staged cuts at 3 announced times + 48 h of real camera). Two arms, same fixture, same seed, same budget:

- **Arm A (shipped Disruptor):** the ring as it stands, plus the added consumer cursor and drop/conservation counters.
- **Arm B (designed SPSC):** the SPSC acquire/release ring built as specified for the spine, with the tile workers funnelled through an explicit MP aggregator into the SPSC refine ring (and the same for the cross-stream feed writer).

**Metric.** `refine_join_p99_ms` — the p99 wall time from the *last* tile of a frame being published into the refine input ring to the refine stage emitting that frame’s verdict. This is the join barrier latency, and it is the only number that prices the protocol at the one junction where the shapes differ. Secondary: `consumer_delivery_violations` (out-of-order or duplicate reads per 100k items, measured by a checksummed consumer cursor) and `shed_accounting_error` (dropped items per the ring counter vs. the ingest receipt ledger).

**Threshold.** Switch to the SPSC design if, over the 48 h window, Arm B’s `refine_join_p99_ms` is **≤ 0.8×** Arm A’s *and* Arm A shows any `consumer_delivery_violations > 0` or `shed_accounting_error > 0` that Arm B does not. The 0.8× is the bar because the Disruptor is already soak-proven; a new ring must be decisively better at the join to justify replacing it and re-running the soak. If Arm A holds `refine_join_p99_ms` within 20% of Arm B, has zero delivery violations once the consumer cursor lands, and accounts every shed, the Disruptor stays and DESIGN.md is amended. If the MP tile workers are, in fact, not concurrent in the real workload (i.e. the pipeline’s sequential driver is the only producer, as `rolling-pipeline` runs today), then the MP junction is theoretical, Arm A’s MP generality is unused, and the SPSC ring would win by default — but that is a measurement of the workload, not of the protocol, and the 48 h soak already suggests the lane is not MP-concurrent in the hot path.
