**Where the budget model lies to the user**

1. **"Spend freely on the front door" is not a stable promise.** The knob compiles to a number of embed-seconds, but the *cost per event* on that stream is not fixed. I-JEPA at 173ms median per tile means ~5.8 tiles/sec of budget, but the diff gate's 54% overall / 88% gradual-scene skip rate is a *ranker, not a separator* — the spec itself admits skipped tiles drift 0.20 vs 0.48 in embedding space. So a stream that gets "quiet" (gradual lighting change, slow pan, a parked car) drifts below the gate and stops consuming budget *while still changing*. The user set "spend freely" and sees low spend; the system interprets low spend as low activity. The knob lies by omission: it meters *embeds issued*, not *change coverage*. A stream can be 100% within budget and 30% covered.

2. **"Sip on the nursery" is a silent precision downgrade, not a throttle.** At low budget, the reflex ranker decides what gets embedded. Rank-order spending means the *cheapest-to-detect* changes win the budget, not the *most meaningful*. A nursery's meaningful events (a child climbing out of crib, a monitor going flat) are often low-magnitude, slow-onset — exactly the tiles the diff gate skips. The user thinks they bought "less attention"; they bought "attention biased toward high-contrast motion." That's a different product.

3. **The 1-false-event/stream/day target is unachievable at low budgets and the UI won't say so.** Precision is a function of embed coverage. At "sip" budget the effective sample rate drops, and the 0.60→1.00 precision gain from Laplacian smoothing was measured on *controlled ground truth* — uncontrolled precision is TO-MEASURE. The trust spec auto-demotes below 0.9 measured precision, but demotion is triggered by *user corrections*, which require the user to notice a miss. Low-budget streams generate fewer events, so fewer corrections, so the demotion loop is starved. The system can sit at 0.7 real precision indefinitely because nobody's tapping.

**Perverse incentives and starvation cases**

- **Rank-order spending is a rich-get-richer scheduler.** Streams with high reflex activity (busy driveway, noisy camera) win budget every tick; quiet streams (nursery at night, a bookshelf cam) never get embedded, so their learned normal never updates, so their diff gate gets *more* stale, so they get *less* budget. The nursery starves not because it's unimportant but because it's boring — until the one night it isn't.
- **The yield ladder starves perception in exactly the hours it's needed.** Library backfill dies first, then reflective, then decision. If the owner runs a long GPU job overnight, decision-tier perception is shed — which is when cameras matter most and when the overnight 5-pass backfill was supposed to run. The completeness % will show backfill stalled; it won't show that live-stream decision coverage also collapsed.
- **Global budget creates cross-stream adversarial coupling.** One stream with a pathological diff pattern (flickering bulb, rain on lens, a fan) can consume the global embed-second pool in reflex-rank order and starve every other lane. There's no per-stream floor in the described model — only a global pool spent by rank. A single broken camera is a denial-of-service on the household's attention budget.
- **"Plain language" knobs hide the exchange rate.** "Spend freely" vs "sip" implies a linear dial. The actual mapping is nonlinear (gate skip rate × tile count × 173ms) and stream-dependent. Two households setting identical knobs get different coverage because their scenes differ. The knob is a vibe, not a contract.
- **Reflective-as-advisor is under-budgeted by design.** At 0.47s/call and demoted to tie-breaker, reflective spend is cheap but its *value* was measured negative as a tie-breaker (precision 1.00→0.75). So the budget line item labeled "reflective" buys something the measurements say hurts. Metering it as a spend category implies it's a lever; it's a liability the yield ladder sheds third.

**What I'd meter and show instead**

1. **Coverage, not spend.** Per stream: "% of changed-tile-seconds that received an embed" and "% of stream-minutes with zero embed coverage." Show the gap between budget spent and change covered. That's the lie made visible.
2. **Per-stream precision with confidence intervals, and a correction-prompt cadence.** Don't wait for organic taps — sample-invite ("was this event real?") at a rate proportional to *unmeasured* precision, so demotion isn't starved. Show "precision unknown (n=3)" rather than a green check.
3. **A per-stream floor, not just a global pool.** Reserve a minimum embed-second allotment per lane before rank-order spending, so one pathological stream can't starve the nursery. Rank-order the *surplus*, not the whole pool.
4. **Gate-drift telemetry.** Track skipped-tile embedding drift over time per stream (the 0.20 vs 0.48 number). When drift on skipped tiles rises, the gate is lying — surface it and force a re-embed sample. This is the single cheapest fix for the "quiet stream starves" failure.
5. **Show the exchange rate.** When the user sets "sip," show the compiled number *and* the predicted coverage ("~40% of changed tiles embedded at current scene activity; precision unknown"). Recompute and show when scene activity changes the effective rate.
6. **Separate "attention" from "safety."** Cameras and alarms shouldn't share a budget pool with library backfill and podcast transcription. The yield ladder already orders them; the *budget* should too, with a non-sheddable tier for streams the user marks as safety-relevant. Otherwise the 4-step ladder will shed decision-tier perception on the front door to protect a backfill the owner forgot about.
7. **Meter reflective honestly.** Label it "advisor (measured to reduce precision when used as tie-breaker)" and default it off, or meter it as a *cost* line the user can zero, not a spend knob they can raise.

The core critique: the model meters *effort* (embed-seconds) and presents it as *attention*, but the user is buying *coverage of change*. Those diverge precisely on the streams that matter most — quiet, slow-onset, low-contrast — and the rank-order scheduler plus the diff-gate ranker compound the divergence. Meter coverage and gate-drift, floor the pool per lane, and stop calling a vibe a knob.
