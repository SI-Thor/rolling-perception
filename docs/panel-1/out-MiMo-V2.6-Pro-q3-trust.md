## What day-1 precision cannot see

**1. Precision is the wrong unit. Trust is broken by the *missed* event, and the spec measures none of it.**  
The trust spec has a precision term (false events/day, 0.9 gate) and **zero recall term**. Day-1 ground truth is controlled and hand-labeled; you never learn what the pipeline silently dropped. Month 2: the front door misses the one time the kid gets home alone. Precision is still 0.97. The 0.20-vs-0.48 embedding drift on skipped tiles is exactly the mechanism — the cheap diff gate is a ranker, and rankers have no lower bound on what they never surface. **Fix:** a mandatory recall probe. Inject synthetic-but-plausible events (a scripted clip played to the camera, or a replayed clip re-fed through the ingest plane) at a known rate — say 1 per stream per 3 days — and measure whether it produced a card. Report recall@probe alongside precision. If probe recall < 0.9, the stream is unhealthy regardless of precision. A system that is quiet because it's correct and a system that is quiet because it's broken are indistinguishable to the user today.

**2. Low-base-rate streams make the 0.9 gate statistically meaningless, and it will fire on noise.**  
Nursery: ~1 true event per week. In 30 days you accumulate maybe 4 labeled positives. One user correction flips measured precision from 1.00 → 0.75. The demotion decision is made on n=4. Meanwhile the front door with 20 events/day can absorb 1 false/day and stay at 0.95. **Fix:** the gate must be a confidence-bound test (Wilson or Beta posterior lower bound), not a point estimate. Require n ≥ 30 labeled events before any demotion, and use a *lower bound* of 0.9 on the posterior — i.e., demote only when you are confident precision is below target, not when the point estimate dips. Otherwise you will demote nursery-cam in week 2 for no reason and the user will read that as "the box is flaky."

**3. The correction signal is biased and non-independent.**  
One-tap corrections only capture false positives the user *saw and bothered to correct*. Users don't correct what they scrolled past; they don't label false negatives at all (can't tap what never appeared). So precision@shown is conditioned on what the attention plane chose to show — a selection effect. Also corrections cluster: a user correcting 5 cards in a row after a bad morning is not 5 independent samples. **Fix:** decay correction weights (exponential, half-life ~1 week) and dedupe bursts. Track *dismissal velocity* (corrections per hour of viewing) as a separate health signal from per-event precision.
