## 1. Where the model lies

**Lie 1: "Embed-seconds" is a unit of value. It is a unit of cost.** 173 ms/tile is the median, not the mean, and not the p99. If a 1080p frame is a 3×3 tile grid, one full frame of decision work is 9 × 173 ms ≈ 1.56 s of embed time. A "sip" that compiles to 30 embed-seconds/day buys you **~19 tiles/hour ≈ one full-frame refresh every 28 minutes**. The A "sip" that compiles to 30 embed-OS (or even a browser tab) has an interruptible, preemptible, work-conserving scheduler that can be reclaimed at any instant. The 4-step yield ladder is a **coarse, non-preemptive** concession: "library backfill dies first" is a kill, not a yield. If the owner opens a game or runs a local LLM at 22:00, the ladder's step 4 (model unload) means the next morning's 5-pass overnight ladder has to cold-start and reload models into 122 GB — and the *owner's work* got priority but the *household's streams* got a cliff, not a ramp. The lie is calling this "instant priority" when it is actually "instant priority with a latency cliff for everything else and a reload tax on resume."

**Lie 3: the plain-language knob is a lossy, non-invertible compression of a real policy.** "Spend freely on the front door" compiles to numbers — but the user cannot invert it: given 30 embed-seconds/day for the nursery, what is the refresh period? What is the smallest event that is reliably detected? The spec never states the compile target. If the knob compiles to a *rate* (embed-seconds/hour), the user is buying a *temporal resolution*, not a *detection quality*. If it compiles to a *recall target* ("detect any 4-tile event lasting ≥5 s"), the cost is wildly different and the knob must show the implied refresh period. Right now the knob is a dial whose units are hidden behind a translation the user cannot audit. Fix: **every knob must render three numbers next to it: refresh period (p50/p95), smallest reliably detected event, and staleness of the learned normal.** If the knob cannot produce those three numbers, it is a mood dial.
