## Route taken — and the two that failed, with evidence

1. **WSL v4l2 — dead end.** `/dev/video*` does not exist in the
   ProArt's WSL. Nothing to capture from.
2. **usbipd — blocked by elevation, as briefed.** usbipd-win is
   installed; `usbipd list` shows the camera (BUSID 4-1,
   3277:0059, "ASUS FHD webcam, ASUS IR camera") in state
   **Not shared**. Sharing (bind) requires an admin prompt; the
   user is absent, so this route was skipped by design.
3. **Windows-side capture — WON, in two attempts.**
   - *VLC attempt (failed):* no ffmpeg.exe exists anywhere on the
     Windows side (PATH, winget, scoop, Program Files, bounded
     find all empty), but VLC is installed. VLC's dshow → HTTP
     MJPEG never produced a listener: startup stalls on a stale
     plugin-cache rebuild, iterations left zombie processes, and
     no frame ever crossed. Abandoned on evidence.
   - *Python + OpenCV (works):* Windows Python 3.12 had no cv2;
     `pip install --user opencv-python-headless` (user scope, no
     admin) added cv2 5.0.0. A small server captured the camera
     (DirectShow idx 0, 1280×720) immediately — but served only to
     Windows-localhost: **the Windows Firewall drops unsolicited
     inbound on the Wi-Fi interface** for processes with no rule,
     from both the LAN (Thor: connect timeout) and WSL (gateway
     IP). A firewall rule needs admin.
   - *Direction flip (the fix):* the camera side dials OUT.
     `C:\Users\casey\proart-push.py` (Windows python.exe PID 42356)
     captures the ASUS FHD webcam and POSTs a JPEG ~2×/s to a
     40-line stdlib receiver on the Thor
     (`~/confluence-play/slice1/push-receiver.py`, PID 651692,
     port 8091), which re-serves `/shot.jpg` and `/stream` on the
     Thor's loopback/LAN. confluence-watch's `shot` ingest polls
     `http://127.0.0.1:8091/shot.jpg` — the exact interface the
     phone will use later; only the transport behind it differs.
     Stream URL shape (no credentials anywhere):
     pusher → `http://192.168.0.78:8091/push` (POST),
     service → `http://127.0.0.1:8091/shot.jpg` (GET).

### Calibration-ageing analysis (frozen stretches excluded)

Hypothesis (from `docs/FINDINGS-CFC-JEPA-V2.md` Finding 2 in the staging repo): on a steady
scene, baseline surprise creeps up toward a fixed trip line as scene detail accumulates, so a
long watch's bound goes stale from underneath. Proxies available (deficit 4): per-6 h windows
anchored at soak start; background = smoothed scores of embedded-but-unchanged tiles inside
verdict frames; near-miss = the largest such score per verdict frame (threshold 0.2500).

**Judgement: (b) — the calibration did not age, on every proxy available.** Median background
surprise does not climb: 0.0817 → 0.0720 → 0.0715 → 0.0880 → 0.0671 (27–35% of the 0.25 line;
the final live window is the *lowest*). Median near-miss is flat at ~0.20 (0.1925–0.2086,
~81% of the line) with no upward trend; p90 background is flat at 0.196–0.205; median
energy_before oscillates (0.43–0.55) with regime, not with elapsed time. The decisive cell is
Nov 3 00:18–06:18: a proven-live, steady deep-night scene produced **zero events in six hours
after ~30 h of running** — under the creep hypothesis that window is exactly where a stale
line should have started firing. It did not. What varies is event *rate*, by regime
(139/live h and 131/live h in the two evening windows; 0 in deep night; 23–66 in daytime),
not the background level.

Clusters — stale line crying wolf, or genuine regime change? **Genuine, localised regime
change, on the tile evidence.** In every cluster the median verdict changes only 2–3 tiles;
77–91% of verdicts change ≤4 tiles; almost none change ≥12 (Nov 2 evening, the most
global, still only 8% ≥12 — a relighting component mixed into an otherwise local pattern).
Changed-tile totals concentrate in tiles 9–15 (tile 14: 543, tile 13: 520, tile 10: 470,
tile 15: 446, tile 9: 414) against 114–160 for tiles 1, 2, 4 — one region of the frame (the
doorway/desk side in the first-frame description) doing the changing, which is what occupancy
and evening activity look like, not whole-frame nuisance. The final cluster (Nov 3 08:57–09:06,
32 events) follows a 70-minute morning quiet and reads as continued morning activity; the
pusher then died *mid-activity* (~09:06:29), not after a quiet stretch. No cluster has the
signature of dense firing off a stale line on a steady scene — the one place that signature
could have appeared, deep night, is silent.

**Bounded-misalignment framing** (`docs/PAPER-LEVERAGE-2610.39302.md`, Balestriero & LeCun,
arXiv:2610.39302 — Levers 2, 3 and 7; conditions in its appendix): the observed behaviour is
**neither** the Findings-2 consumption pattern **nor** a break of the bound on live data.
Bounded misalignment being consumed would show steady-scene surprise climbing toward the
trip line as detail accumulates; the medians above are flat-to-falling and the trip line was
never approached by background (p90 ≈ 0.20 of a 0.25 threshold throughout — close, but
stationary, not climbing). A genuine break would show background distributions jumping and
staying displaced; instead each regime shift (day ↔ evening ↔ deep night — different induced
kernels, Lever 7) moved the event *rate* while background levels re-centred to the same
0.07–0.09 band, which is the Lever-2 picture: ranking survives kernel shift, absolute
calibration is per-regime. The one true bound-break in this run is not representational at
all: during the freezes the input stopped being a view of the scene, so no misalignment
bound applies — and the build stamped that silence meaningful anyway (deficit 3).
Per-tile ρ̂ (Lever 3's falsifier for this analysis) cannot be estimated from this ledger —
it needs repeated steady observations, which the verdict-only log does not retain; recorded
as an open measurement for the post-verdict iteration, alongside the already-chartered
CfC-style predictor as candidate expectation layer and the judge's recorded-advisor-only
seat (Findings 1 and 3), neither of which was touched during the soak.
