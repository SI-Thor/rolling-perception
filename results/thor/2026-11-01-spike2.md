## 1. Three arrangements, same kernel, same slices

Kernel identical in all three: `out[i] = reps × (v*1.000001 + 1e-7)` over the
slice (exp1's `lane_work`). Slice sizes: **small** N=65,536 reps=20;
**big** N=1,048,576 reps=60.

Definitions, stated before the numbers because the bases differ:

- **launch** — per-slice launch on one stream, host waits for each slice.
  Pickup = GPU-event elapsed, submit-event (idle timing stream, recorded at
  submission) → begin-event (work stream, immediately before the kernel).
  This excludes host launch-call time.
- **shadow** — two streams, host stays one slice ahead per stream (submit
  next on a stream only after collecting that stream's previous slice, so
  compute on A overlaps staging/launch of B and vice versa). Same pickup
  definition. This is a shallow pipeline, not a deep queue — by construction
  each stream is idle at its next submission, which is why its pickup stays
  at launch levels.
- **persistent** — one resident kernel, 2 blocks × 256 threads, polling a
  control struct in host-pinned mapped memory (exp3 shape). Pickup =
  host-clock time from raising `request_seq` to observing `started_seq`
  (which the kernel writes before starting work) — i.e. it *includes* the
  mapped-memory round trip. Work = started → `done_seq` observed.
  The persistent work pass uses only its resident 512 threads (grid-stride),
  vs the full grid (256 blocks small / 4096 blocks big) for launch/shadow.
  That asymmetry is not a rigging — it *is* the persistent proposition at
  neighbour-friendly occupancy, and §1.1 prices it.

| Arrangement | Slices | Pickup p50 / p95 / p99 (µs) | Work p50 / p95 (µs) | Total wall | Host CPU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| launch, small | 300 | **1.1 / 1.2 / 1.2** | 6.1 / 7.2 | 4.2 ms | 4.2 ms |
| shadow, small | 300 | **1.1 / 1.1 / 1.1** | 9.7 / 10.0 | **2.7 ms** | 2.7 ms |
| persistent, small | 60 | 9.2 / 10.1 / 10.4 | 56.9 / 72.5 | 4.1 ms (60 slices) | 4.0 ms |
| launch, big | 60 | **1.1 / 1.9 / 9.1** | 51.8 / 56.0 | 3.6 ms | 3.6 ms |
| shadow, big | 60 | **1.1 / 1.1 / 9.1** | 90.5 / 91.0 | **3.0 ms** | 3.0 ms |
| persistent, big | 20 | 4.3 / 11.0 / 11.0 | 1427 / 2100 | 27.1 ms (20 slices) | 27.0 ms |

### 1.1 Reading the table (the honest conclusions)

1. **Per-slice launch pickup is ~1 µs p50 on this box** (GPU-side), with a
   p99 tail of 9.1 µs on big slices. The premise that launch overhead is the
   problem rolling perception must escape does not survive measurement at
   these slice sizes — the driver/stream path is already fast.
2. **The 2-block persistent kernel loses on both axes.** Its pickup
   (4–9 µs p50, host round-trip basis) is not better than launch pickup,
   and its work pass is **9× slower (small) to 27× slower (big)** because
   512 resident threads on 2 SMs cannot do the work of a full grid. Per-slice
   end-to-end: big launch ≈ 60 µs vs persistent ≈ 1,355 µs. Persistent
   kernels would need a much larger resident grid to compete on work, which
   is precisely the neighbour cost the 2-block cap exists to avoid.
3. **Shadow/pipelining trades per-kernel time for total time.** Co-running
   two streams inflates each kernel's duration (6.1→9.7 µs small,
   51.8→90.5 µs big — SM sharing is real) yet total wall still improves
   (4.2→2.7 ms small, 3.6→3.0 ms big) because launch/staging latency is
   hidden behind compute. Modest, real, and worth keeping as the default
   arrangement shape.
4. **Host CPU is ~100% of wall in every arrangement as implemented.** For
   launch/shadow the synchronising host thread spins (default
   `cudaEventSynchronize` behaviour); for persistent the host explicitly
   busy-polls the ack. So "CPU cost" does not differentiate the arrangements
   here — all three burn one host core while waiting. A blocking-sync /
   yield variant would change CPU cost at some latency cost; not measured
   here, recorded as an open follow-up, not asserted.

**Spike-2 arrangement verdict (per PLAN §6, keep whichever the numbers
favour): arrangement (a) launch is the pickup/work winner, (b) shadow is
the total-wall winner and the recommended default; (c) persistent at
2 blocks is not competitive for slice work — its remaining niche is the
sub-10 µs control/ack path (exp3: 4.8 µs mean on a 1024-element buffer),
not perception slices.**

## 2. Stream priorities vs the rolling staircase (big lanes, simultaneous arrival)

exp1's rolling staircase came from *staggered* arrivals. This experiment
removes the stagger: 4 big lanes (N=1M, reps=60) launched back-to-back on
4 streams, completion from CUDA-event GPU timestamps, 5 interleaved trials
per config, run twice end-to-end (identical to 0.01 ms both times).
Priority range on the box: greatest=−5, least=0; PRIORITY assigns lane 0
the greatest priority, decreasing by lane.

| Config | L0 | L1 | L2 | L3 | Median spread (last−first) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Default streams | 0.18 ms | 0.34 ms | 0.50 ms | 0.65 ms | 0.47 ms |
| Stream priorities | 0.19 ms | 0.34 ms | 0.50 ms | 0.66 ms | 0.47 ms |

**Finding — priorities change nothing here, because there is nothing for
them to fix.** Even at simultaneous arrival, the lanes do *not* fair-share
and finish together: they complete in a clean staircase in submission
order (~0.16 ms apart) under default streams already. The block scheduler
dispatches the earlier stream's blocks first at this grid size; priority
hints neither sharpen nor flatten the staircase (every lane within
0.01 ms of its default-config time). The "concurrent kernels bunch up"
picture came from the first, buggy host-observation reading of exp1;
with event timestamps, on this box and kernel, it does not occur in
either arrival pattern. Priority plumbing is therefore **not** a
dependency of the rolling design — submission order is the mechanism.

## 3. Bounded soak — persistent kernel, exactly 10 minutes

Config: `spike2_soak 600` — the exp3-shape persistent kernel, 2 blocks ×
256 threads, host issuing one 1024-element work request every 100 ms and
busy-polling the ack; `tegrastats --interval 10000` for the full run
(60 samples, `soak_telemetry.log`). Wrapper: `run_soak.sh`. Wall time
600.1 s, exit code 0, **5,981 requests served, final buffer 5981.0 =
expected, MATCH**, stop via flag, and the wrapper's bracket-trick pgrep
check printed **GONE** (re-verified from a fresh SSH session afterwards).

Ack latency over the soak: **p50 5.4 µs** (steady state; the first 30 s
bucket's p50 of 1,139 µs is launch/boot transient under a busy GPU),
**p95 1,829.6 µs, p99 2,070 µs, max 10,497.9 µs, mean 177.5 µs**.
The honest contrast with exp3: in a tight no-sleep loop on an idle box
the same path measured p95 5.5 µs; at a realistic 100 ms request cadence
with the host sleeping between requests, the median stays at exp3 level
but ~5% of acks land in a ~2 ms tail. Median: excellent. Tail: a real
property of the cadence + host wakeup path, reported, not hidden.

Thermal / power (tegrastats, 60 samples over 590 s):

| Quantity | Before | Peak during | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPU temp (gpu@ zone) | 39.3 °C | **46.4 °C** | 46.4 °C (nvidia-smi 45 °C) |
| tj / cpu@ temp | 39.3 / 37.7 °C | 52.1 / 52.1 °C | ~51.5 °C |
| VDD_GPU | 18.9 W | 27.9 W (first 200 s) → 7.9–9.0 W thereafter | — |
| VIN (system) | 58.9 W | 71.7 W | 59.2 W |
| GPU util (nvidia-smi) | 98% (!) | — | 35% |
| Load average (1 min) | 5.83 | — | 76.90 |

**Attribution, carefully — the soak was disturbed, not the disturber.**
The box was already busy when the soak started (GPU 98% utilised by
other work at 14:59, load ~5.8). At ~160 s into the soak a foreign
**all-core CPU load** arrived: mean CPU utilisation 7% → 46% → 53% →
62% → 99.5% → 99.6% by 100 s buckets, all 14 cores at 2,601 MHz,
`VDD_CPU_SOC_MSS` up to ~34 W, which is what drives the tj/cpu@ rise to
52 °C and VIN's 71.7 W peak. Our soak cannot produce that: its host
thread sleeps 100 ms between requests and its GPU side is 2 spinning
blocks. Evidence for the soak's own footprint is in the VDD_GPU trace:
once the foreign GPU job ended (~200 s), VDD_GPU settled at **7.9–9.0 W
including our resident kernel**, and GPU temperature peaked at only
46.4 °C even while the SoC around it was being heated to 52 °C by the
CPU burn. Nothing belonging to the box was stopped, starved, or crashed
by the soak — the soak's own ack tail (§ above) is where the neighbour's
load is visible, i.e. the interference ran the other way.

**Soak verdict:** a 2-block persistent kernel can sit resident on this
shared Thor for 10 minutes, serve ~6,000 requests at a 100 ms cadence,
hold a 5.4 µs median ack, and stop cleanly on command — with a GPU-side
power footprint in single-digit watts above idle and no thermal event.
What 10 minutes does *not* prove (per FEASIBILITY.md): hours-long
behaviour, watchdog/preemption over longer horizons, and the tail under
load deserves a repeat on a quiet box before anyone quotes p95.

## 4. Summary for the parent lane

- **Arrangements:** launch (pickup p50 1.1 µs) and two-stream shadow
  (best total wall) both beat the 2-block persistent kernel for slice
  work — persistent work is 9–27× slower at neighbour-friendly
  occupancy, and its pickup is not better. Persistent's proven niche is
  the tiny-buffer control/ack path only.
- **Priorities:** no measurable effect (±0.01 ms); the rolling staircase
  under simultaneous arrival comes from submission order, not priority
  hints. Not a design dependency.
- **Soak:** 10 min resident, 5,981 requests, buffer exact, clean flagged
  stop, process verified gone, GPU ≤46.4 °C, own power footprint small;
  box's other work disturbed the soak's tail, not vice versa.
- Logs kept with this file: `soak_run.log`, `soak_telemetry.log`,
  `soak_summary.log`.
